Case file · Scope

Cluedle

Wordle for armchair detectives: a daily, hand-written mystery you solve by naming who did it and why, in five guesses or fewer.

Approved

The unique kernelRead closely, don't guess your way through

A daily case you crack by reading closely. You have to find the puzzle to solve the puzzle: an encrypted clue is hidden somewhere on the board, and the key is hidden in the story. Some witnesses lie, so you also have to decide what to trust. All of it lives on a murder board that makes you feel like a detective working a case, not someone filling in a form.

How it fits togetherPlayer → core loop → proof

Open today's case: a newspaper-clipping story on the corkboard, with six suspect cards pinned up.

An encrypted clue is hidden somewhere on the board. Spot it, find its key in the story, and it unscrambles, any time and without spending a guess. Skimmers may never notice it.

Name who did it and why. Feedback says right suspect, right motive, both, or neither.

A wrong guess pins up a new plain clue. Some are witness statements, and one witness is lying.

Name the right suspect and motive within five guesses to earn a detective rank, then copy the share card. Miss five times and the case goes cold.

Who it's forArmchair detectives

People who play a daily puzzle like Wordle and love a good mystery. They want a small, satisfying "I solved it" moment each day that they can share. Today they get that from word puzzles, or from mystery novels and shows that take hours instead of minutes.

Inspiration & identityFeels like working a case

  • Wordle: one puzzle a day, the same for everyone, an emoji result card.
  • Detective murder boards: corkboard, pinned suspect cards, newspaper clippings, red string.
  • The board, not dropdowns and forms, is what makes it feel like a case.

What "working" looks likeThe finish line

A static web app opened in a browser shows today's case on a murder board. In one sitting a player reads the story, spots the hidden encrypted clue and cracks it using a key in the story, takes a wrong guess and sees a new clue pinned up, catches a lying witness, solves the case, earns a detective rank, and copies the share card to the clipboard.

The "oh, that's cool" beat: noticing the hidden clue, then watching it unscramble once you spot its key in the story.

Why it matters: an entry for Devpost "Build With AI: Basics." Judges watch a 1–3 minute demo, so "a small, polished, complete experience beats an ambitious half-built one."

The boundaryNow, later, cut

Now In the proof of concept
  • Three polished, hand-written cases (Claude drafts, you edit), with the daily case chosen by date.
  • Each case: a story, 6 suspects, 6 motives, 6 witness statements with exactly one liar, plain clues that unlock on wrong guesses, one or two encrypted clues hidden on the board with their key hidden in the story.
  • Guess who and why; feedback per guess (suspect / motive / both / neither); five guesses max, or the case goes cold.
  • Finding and cracking an encrypted clue, at any time, reveals it without spending a guess. A player who never finds it can still solve the case.
  • A detective rank (e.g. Amateur up to Seasoned Detective) based on guesses used, cipher found and cracked, and liar caught. Exact ranks set in the PRD.
  • Murder-board look: corkboard, pinned suspect cards, newspaper-clipping story, clues pinned as they unlock.
  • Red string the player draws between pins to track their own thinking.
  • Emoji share card copied to the clipboard, with the detective rank and a 🔓 for a cracked cipher. Style chosen from options in the PRD.
  • No AI at runtime; everything is hand-written and rule-based.
Later Worth doing, not now
  • Red-string physics: string that sags, sways, or reacts as clues are pinned.
  • Correct red-string links counting toward the detective rank (needs the game to know which links are "correct").
  • Cases four through seven.
Cut Named, with a reason
  • Making the accusation by dragging red string. The string tracks evidence the way detectives in stories use it; it isn't the guess control.
